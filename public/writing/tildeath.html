‘Til Death Do We Part Didn’t Account For Miraculous Return to Life

Word Count: 1,100 // Posted: 03/19/2025

Gendo Ikari has won and gotten what he wanted all along: his wife to return to him. But what he forgot was there would be consequences to his actions, something his wife hadn't forgotten.

Gendo Ikari had won.

The means in which he had averted the wiles of SEELE and fate itself—which in any other case would have relinquished control to his son instead—was not important. He had brought all of humanity together into Instrumentality through Third Impact then, once his beloved had joined the collective, he brought them all back out.

(Though, he had no qualms about leaving SEELE in metaphysical limbo. It’s what they wanted after all.)

So when Gendo Ikari awoke once more in the depths of Terminal Dogma, two important things happened. The first was that he could tell the missing piece of his heart had clicked back into place and that all was right in the world. So he jumped to his feet and rushed out, intending to get to the loading bay of Unit 01, where his wife and son had most certainly manifested.

The second important thing, the one thing he had missed, was the one he had deemed a tragic but necessary sacrifice had been floating in the LCL behind him, just below the now empty crimson cross. And she was gasping to life.

He all but skidded into Unit 01’s loading bay where he found his son hovering over and fussing over a nude woman he knew was his wife. He stopped some feet away and murmured, “Yui…”

She hacked up some LCL and looked over at him, brown eyes burning surprisingly hot. Shinji had gone rigid, staring at him with wide eyes. She stared at him for a few moments longer and, without looking away, shooed Shinji away, “Shinji, baby. Go find your momma some clothes.”

The boy recognized the second purpose of this; that she was telling him it was time for the adults to talk. He nodded stiffly and rushed off, “Y-yes, mother!”

She finally turned her fire-hot gaze off him to watch her son go. She mused, “’Mother’...do you have him call you ‘father’?”

“I didn’t tell him he had to.”

She turned to stare now at the inert form of Unit 01, which had somehow returned to its place. Its eyes had gone dark, indicating its soul was elsewhere—namely, right in front of it, where it belonged.

With a dead tone, Yui uttered five terrible words, “Gendo, I want a divorce.”

The world was no longer right as the missing piece clicked once more out of place. His heart plummeted to his feet and everything went cold. He croaked, “Wh-what? Why…?”

“Need I tell you every reason or just the big ones?” Yui hissed, her tone dangerous.

She picked herself up off the concrete floor, smacked herself in the chest in a few times which made her hack up what LCL was left in her lungs. She made no attempt to hide her nudity from him—after all, it wasn’t anything he hadn’t seen before. But it was soon something he would never see again.

Meanwhile, Gendo opened and closed his jaw like a fish, unable to form the words, or even thoughts, for a reply. Seeing him like this, Yui rolled her eyes and began to count on her hands, “You abandoned our son, treated him like a mere soldier, shunted him off to live with a strange woman—“

“Major Katsuragi offered to take him in.”

He went quiet as her gaze once fell on him. He withered under it, like an ant under a magnifying glass. She scowled, “Not my point. Let’s see, what else...didn’t bother stopping Third Impact! And also used what little you could salvage of me to make some...some...clone daughter!”

“It was all necessary!” Gendo cried, “I did what I was supposed to do!”

“What you were supposed to do was stop the old men from ending the world!” she snapped.

“How would I have known if you never told me?!”

“Because I expected you to do what was best for Shinji!”

“He needed his mother!”

“And he didn’t need his father?!”

“Um.”

The pair grew silent as both looked to the other end of the catwalk where Shinji stood, very pointedly not looking at his very nude mother but holding out some clothes. Yui’s gaze softened when it fell on her son and she crossed the catwalk to take them from him. She patted his cheek, “Good boy. Now go find your sister.”

“My...sister?”

“Yes, Rei. She’s probably still down in the basement—where your father left her—and very frightened.”

He nodded quickly and rushed off once more, probably just as much to do what she asked as to let his parents continue their argument in peace. Yui watched him go with a fond expression before she turned it back on her very soon-to-be-ex-husband where it hardened once more. “Where was I...right! I want a divorce and I’m taking full custody of both children.”

“Full...both?” he gasped, “I don’t get to see either of them?!”

“It’s not like you bothered to see them while I was MIA,” she scowled as she began to get dressed, once more not caring that he was in the room, “I might consider working something out if you show me you can change.”

“You might?” he asked, a tiny sliver of hope entering his heart.

“After all, to be human is to change. I’d like to think you’re not completely irredeemable.”

She turned around, finger held up, “I also want child support! I am legally dead after all and that will surely be quite the money pit to get that resolved.”

“It’s unprecedented,” her husband grumbled.

“Exciting,” her eyes shone, “I’m the first woman to ever “died” and come back to life. I suppose that might resolve the divorce issue after all. Our marriage might be null and void anyway.”

“’Til death do we part’,” he quoted at her but she only waved him off.

“You know that just a ceremony! Pomp and circumstance!”

Gendo wondered if it would be worth it to make a case that, since she was alive once more and had never gotten divorced, their marriage license would return to effect. Maybe not, that would just piss her off further. He was at least still a tiny, little, minuscule bit still in her good graces. Better to play nice and at least be able to stay there. There was better than being completely without her.

“Of course, this is all assuming you don’t get arrested the second the UN, Japanese government, whoever gets here! You did end the world, after all.”

He groaned. Maybe he should have just left everyone in Instrumentality after all.
